Literature Review: Shape Memory Alloys and Origami Structures within Soft Robotics

The literature review focused on key enabling technologies in soft robotics, specifically shape memory alloys (SMAs), pneumatic actuators, and origami-inspired structural design. It examined the functional characteristics of SMAs—such as the shape memory effect and pseudoelasticity—as compact and lightweight actuation methods, and contrasted them with the high-force capabilities of pneumatic actuators. Origami structures were explored for their ability to encode complex behaviors, enable reconfigurable forms, and enhance mechanical performance. The review identified a significant research gap in the hybrid integration of these actuation strategies, suggesting strong potential for combining their complementary strengths in modular, scalable systems.

Prototype: Novel Tendon Driven Kresling Origami Actuator

In parallel with the literature review, a functional prototype was developed over a two-week period to explore the practical application of origami-based actuation. The actuator consists of stacked Kresling origami units fabricated from laser-cut polypropylene, with tendon-driven motion provided by 5V stepper motors. The design achieved consistent vertical compression—up to 36% of its extended height—and programmable bending motion through tendon actuation. While the chosen motors limited overall torque, the prototype demonstrated the feasibility of modular stacking and programmable motion. This hands-on development validated insights from the literature and provides a foundation for future exploration of hybrid soft actuators incorporating SMAs and pneumatic components.
